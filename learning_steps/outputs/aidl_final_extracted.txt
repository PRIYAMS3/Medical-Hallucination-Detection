Abstract—Phishing websites continue to pose a critical threat
to cybersecurity, and machine-learning-based detection systems
frequently suffer performance degradation when deployed on
data distributions that differ from their training environment.
This paper presents a confidence-aware hybrid ensemble frame-
work that combines soft-voting neural classifiers with a rule-
based override mechanism to improve detection robustness un-
der distribution shift. Three artificial neural network (ANN)
architectures—a compact two-layer network, a deeper three-
layer network, and a regularised dropout variant—are trained
on 30 engineered URL and webpage features extracted from
11,055 labelled samples and fused via probability-level soft voting.
A novel hybrid decision layer monitors prediction confidence
and activates domain-informed heuristic rules when confidence
falls below a calibrated threshold, selectively overriding low-
confidence predictions toward the phishing class. Five-fold strat-
ified cross-validation on the internal dataset yields an ensemble
F1-score of0.9704±0.0026and ROC-AUC of0.9954±0.0006.
On an external dataset of 2,456 samples—requiring feature har-
monisation across 16 aligned attributes—the hybrid mechanism
increases recall from0.8201to0.8473(+2.72percentage points)
and F1-score from0.8820to0.8956(+1.37percentage points) rel-
ative to the base ensemble, demonstrating meaningful robustness
gains. The complete pipeline is packaged for both batch inference
and lightweight REST API deployment, making it practical for
integration into browser extensions, security operations centre
(SOC) workflows, and enterprise URL screening systems.
Index Terms—phishing detection, ensemble learning,

I. INTRODUCTION
Phishing attacks remain among the most pervasive and
financially damaging cybersecurity threats. The Anti-Phishing
Working Group (APWG) reported over 4.7 million phishing at-
tacks in 2023 alone, representing a sustained upward trend [1].
These attacks exploit user trust through deceptive websites
that impersonate legitimate services, harvesting credentials,
financial data, and personally identifiable information. Despite
advances in machine learning (ML) for automated phishing
detection, two persistent challenges limit the real-world relia-
bility of current systems.
First, most ML-based detectors are evaluated exclusively
under in-distribution (ID) conditions, where training and
test data share the same statistical properties. When de-
ployed against novel phishing campaigns—featuring unseen
URL patterns, shifted feature distributions, or new eva-
sion techniques—these models experience significant perfor-
mance degradation, a phenomenon broadly termeddistribu-
tion shift[2]–[4].Second, purely data-driven models lack a
principled mechanism for signalling uncertainty. A model that
outputs a confident but incorrect prediction is arguably more
dangerous than one that abstains or defers, yet few phishing
detection pipelines incorporate prediction confidence into their
decision logic [5], [6].
Recent literature has explored several directions to address
these limitations: deep architectures such as CNNs and LSTMs
for richer feature extraction [7], [8], transformer-based mod-
els leveraging pre-trained language representations [9]–[11],
ensemble methods combining heterogeneous classifiers [6],
[12], and explainability-focused frameworks [5]. While these
approaches improve accuracy on benchmark datasets, they
generally do not address the joint problem of maintaining
robustness under distribution shiftandproviding actionable
confidence estimates for deployment-time decision-making.
This paper addresses the identified gap by proposing a
confidence-aware hybrid ensembleframework that integrates
neural soft-voting ensemble prediction with selective rule-
based override logic. The key insight is that when a neural
ensemble is uncertain—quantified by a calibrated confidence
score—domain-informed heuristic rules can safely override the
model’s prediction toward the phishing class, improving recall
in precisely those regions of the feature space where the model
is least reliable.

II. RELATEDWORK
Phishing detection research spans classical machine learn-
ing, deep learning, ensemble methods, and hybrid paradigms.
This section reviews key developments organised by method-
ology, with emphasis on recent contributions (2023–2026).
A. Classical Machine Learning Approaches
Traditional approaches employ hand-crafted URL and web-
page features with classifiers such as Random Forest, Gradient
Boosting, and Support Vector Machines. Reyesdortaet al.[13]
systematically evaluated multiple ML classifiers on malicious
URL features, reporting Random Forest as a strong baseline.
Prasadet al.[14] proposed PhiUSIIL, a framework combining
similarity indices with incremental learning to handle evolving
phishing patterns. Nagyet al.[12] compared sequential and
parallel ML pipelines, demonstrating that feature engineering
quality often matters more than classifier choice. Kustiawan
et al.[15] conducted a systematic review of feature engi-
neering techniques for phishing URL detection, identifying
URL structure, domain registration, and SSL characteristics as
the most discriminative feature groups. While these methods
achieve high accuracy on curated benchmarks, they generally
lack mechanisms for quantifying prediction uncertainty and
adapting to distributional changes.
B. Deep Learning Approaches
Deep learning architectures have shown promise for auto-
matic feature extraction from raw URLs and webpage content.
Haqet al.[7] employed convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
for character-level URL analysis, while Aslamet al.[8] pro-
posed AntiPhishStack, an LSTM-based stacked generalisation
model. Heet al.[16] introduced feature extension mechanisms
for enriching URL representations prior to classification. Zaimi
et al.[17] combined deep learning with SMOTE-Tomek link
resampling to handle class imbalance. Bariket al.[18] applied
optimisation-driven deep learning architectures to phishing
URL detection, and Bourigueet al.[19] proposed a clustering-
enhanced deep model. While these deep approaches excel at
capturing complex nonlinear patterns, they typically require
larger training sets and are evaluated only under in-distribution
settings.
C. Transformer and Language Model Approaches
Transformer-based models have been adapted for phishing
detection to leverage contextual embeddings of URL tokens.
Suet al.[10] applied BERT-based architectures to malicious
URL identification, and Heet al.[9] proposed a Tiny-
BERT stacking method that distils transformer representations
for efficient inference. Remyaet al.[11] introduced BGL-
PhishNet, a hybrid combining BERT, Graph Neural Networks,
and LightGBM. Kibriyaet al.[20] explored lightweight LLM-
based approaches for malicious URL detection. Huynhet
al.[21] proposed a semantic-aware approach leveraging con-
textual URL semantics. These models achieve state-of-the-art
accuracy but incur higher computational costs and may overfit
to the linguistic characteristics of training-time URLs.
D. Ensemble and Hybrid Methods
Ensemble learning has been widely adopted to combine
heterogeneous models for improved generalisation. Zhouet
al.[6] proposed EnLeM, an ensemble learning-based model
using multiple classifier fusion. Zhouet al.[22] combined
metaheuristic algorithms with ensemble classifiers for im-
proved detection. Turket al.[23] explored optimisation-
supported deep learning ensembles, and Alorvoret al.[24]
proposed temporal convolutional networks for real-time brand
protection. Zhouet al.[25] introduced a logic-constraint neural
network framework that integrates domain knowledge as soft
constraints during training. Dandotiyaet al.[26] developed a
real-time phishing identification system through ML-enhanced
browser extensions, while Gobinathet al.[27] proposed opti-
mised URL intelligence for deep learning-based classification.
E. Distribution Shift and Domain Adaptation
A critical gap in the literature concerns robustness under
distribution shift. Rashidet al.[2] proposed unsupervised
domain adaptation for phishing URL detection generalisation,
representing one of the few works explicitly addressing this
challenge. Komosnyet al.[3] studied phishing detection across
European non-English languages, revealing significant cross-
lingual performance degradation. Duarteet al.[4] examined
phishing detection in parked domains within financial institu-
tions, demonstrating the domain-specific nature of distribution
shift.
F . Research Gap and Positioning
Table I summarises the positioning of the proposed work
relative to key prior contributions. Most existing systems
address either classifier architecture or feature engineering
but do not jointly tackle confidence-aware decision-making
and distribution-shift robustness. Our work fills this gap by
introducing a lightweight, deployment-ready confidence-aware
override layer atop a neural soft-voting ensemble, with explicit
cross-dataset validation.

B. Neural Network Architectures
Three ANN architectures with varying capacity and regu-

ModelM 1 (Simple ANN):A compact two-layer network

D. Confidence-Aware Hybrid Decision Layer
The core novelty of this work lies in the confidence-aware
hybrid decision mechanism that augments the neural ensemble
with selective rule-based overrides. The prediction confidence
is defined as:
C(x) = max (P(y= 1|x),1−P(y= 1|x))(6)
This confidence score ranges from0.5(maximum uncer-
tainty) to1.0(maximum certainty). WhenC(x)< τ(where
τ= 0.8is a calibrated threshold), the system engages a
domain-informed rule engine.
The rule engine evaluates four expert-defined heuristic con-
ditions on the input feature vector:

These rules are derived from well-established indicators in
the phishing detection literature [1], [15]. The hybrid decision
is formalised in Algorithm 1.
The design rationale is conservative: the override only
activates whenall three conditionsare met—low confidence, at
least one rule triggered, and the model predicts legitimate. This
prevents the rule engine from interfering with high-confidence
model decisions, preserving precision while improving recall
in uncertain regions.

IV. EXPERIMENTALSETUP
A. Evaluation Protocol
Two evaluation protocols are employed:
Internal evaluation:Five-fold stratified cross-validation
on the primary dataset (N= 11,055), ensuring each fold
preserves the class distribution. All three base models and the
ensemble are trained and evaluated independently in each fold.
External evaluation:The ensemble and hybrid models,
trained on the full internal dataset, are evaluated on the exter-
nal ARFF dataset (N ext = 2,456) after feature harmonisation.
This protocol explicitly tests generalisation under distribution
shift—the external data originates from a different collection
period and labelling methodology.
B. Performance Metrics
Five standard classification metrics are reported:
•Accuracy:Overall fraction of correct predictions.
•Precision:TP/(TP+FP)— measures the reliability of
phishing alerts.
•Recall:TP/(TP+FN)— measures the completeness of
phishing detection, critical for security applications.
•F1-score:Harmonic mean of precision and recall.
•ROC-AUC:Area under the receiver operating charac-
teristic curve, measuring discriminative ability across all
thresholds.
C. Statistical Testing
To assess the statistical significance of performance dif-
ferences between models, pairwise exact sign-flip tests are
conducted across cross-validation folds with Holm correction
for multiple comparisons. Givenk= 5folds,p-values are
inherently conservative; we therefore report both statistical
significance and effect sizes (Cohen’sd z).

B. Practical Implications
For deployment in security-critical environments, the hybrid
mode offers an important safety margin. In phishing detection,
false negatives (missed phishing sites) are typically more
costly than false positives (legitimate sites flagged for review).
The hybrid mechanism’s emphasis on recall improvement
aligns with this asymmetric cost structure. Organisations can
select between ensemble mode (balanced performance) and
hybrid mode (safety-first operation) based on their risk toler-
ance.
The lightweight API deployment architecture enables inte-
gration into diverse operational contexts: browser extensions
for end-user protection, email gateway filtering for enterprise
security, SOC triage workflows for threat analysis, and DNS-
level blocking for network-wide protection.
C. Limitations
Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the
ternary feature encoding ({−1,0,1}) discretises continuous
signals, potentially losing discriminative information. Future
work could explore continuous feature representations. Sec-
ond, the rule engine comprises only four heuristic rules; while
these are well-established in the literature, a more compre-
hensive rule set or learned rule discovery mechanism could
improve override effectiveness. Third, the confidence threshold
τ= 0.8was selected empirically; a principled threshold

selection method (e.g., based on expected calibration error
or cost-sensitive optimisation) would strengthen the approach.
Fourth, the external evaluation uses a single external dataset;
validation on multiple diverse datasets from different time
periods would provide stronger evidence of generalisation.
Fifth, the statistical significance analysis is limited by the small
number of cross-validation folds (k= 5).
D. Comparison with State-of-the-Art
Direct numerical comparison with prior work is constrained
by differences in datasets, feature sets, and evaluation pro-
tocols. However, the proposed system’s internal F1-score
(0.9704) and ROC-AUC (0.9954) are competitive with recent
results reported by Zhouet al.[6] and Bariket al.[18].
Crucially, the proposed system’s explicit external validation
and confidence-aware override mechanism address aspects that
most prior work does not evaluate.
VII. CONCLUSION ANDFUTUREWORK
This paper presented a confidence-aware hybrid ensemble
framework for phishing website detection that addresses the
dual challenges of achieving high in-distribution accuracy and
maintaining robustness under distribution shift. The proposed
system integrates three neural network classifiers via soft-
voting ensemble prediction and augments the ensemble with
a confidence-aware rule-based override mechanism that selec-
tively intervenes when model confidence is low.
Experimental evaluation demonstrated that the ensemble
achieves an F1-score of0.9704and ROC-AUC of0.9954
under five-fold cross-validation on the internal dataset. Under
distribution shift, the hybrid mechanism improves recall by
+2.72percentage points and F1-score by+1.37percentage
points on an external dataset of2,456samples, with only
1.79%of predictions requiring rule override. The system
is packaged as a deployment-ready pipeline supporting both
batch and API-based inference.
Future research directions include: (i)temporal drift adap-
tation—incorporating online learning or periodic recalibration
to handle evolving phishing campaigns over time; (ii)au-
tomated rule discovery—using interpretable ML techniques
(e.g., LIME, SHAP) to generate data-driven rules that com-
plement expert-defined heuristics; (iii)richer confidence esti-
mation—exploring Bayesian neural networks or Monte Carlo
dropout for uncertainty quantification; (iv)multi-dataset eval-
uation—validating on temporally separated datasets from mul-
tiple phishing campaign waves; and (v)continuous online cal-
ibration—implementing calibration monitoring and adaptation
in the deployed system.
